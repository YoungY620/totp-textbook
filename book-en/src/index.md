# One-Time Passwords, Authenticators, and Password Managers

This book follows a single technical thread: the **time-based one-time password (TOTP)** — the
six-digit code that ticks over every 30 seconds in the authenticator app on your phone — from its
cryptographic beginnings to the ecosystem it anchors today, and the deeper problems it shares with
**password managers**.

Every chapter is built around one **primary source** (a paper or a standard), ordered by time, and
ends with a short "related work" section so you can follow the thread further.

## The through-line

1. **The idea**: in 1981, Lamport proposed one-time passwords from hash chains, requiring no secret on the server.
2. **Engineering**: RFC 1760's S/KEY brought that idea to early Unix systems.
3. **Standardization**: RFC 4226 (HOTP) replaced hash chains with HMAC and gave a provable security analysis;
   RFC 6238 (TOTP) swapped the counter for a time step and became the standard behind every authenticator app.
4. **Improvement**: T/Key (CCS 2017) tried to bring Lamport's "no secret on the server" back to TOTP.
5. **Real-world failure**: the algorithm holds up; implementations and backups do not — key storage,
   clock manipulation, phishing, and account recovery.
6. **Password managers**: from 2014 to 2026, researchers kept showing the gap between "zero-knowledge
   encryption" marketing and actual security.
7. **The way out**: phishing-resistant WebAuthn / passkeys are displacing the shared-secret model.
